Privacy

Triangle Tots is published by Danford Enterprises, LLC. Here is everything this site keeps, and it isn't much.

Daily visit counts

Each day we count page views by site, page path and the referring website's hostname. We count taps on a few buttons the same way, by the button's name and nothing more, and a joined list is counted as a tap. That's a tally, not a record of anyone.

  • No cookie, no IP address stored, no user agent and no per-visitor id.
  • Your address is held in memory for up to an hour, only to limit abuse, and is never written to disk.
  • Daily counts are kept, so we can compare this week with the same week in other years.

The email lists

If you join the Sunday plan or the family waitlist, we keep your email address, which list you joined, the area if you give one, and the date.

  • It's kept apart from Tot Routines family data and never linked to it.
  • It's never sold, and never shared except with the sending service that delivers the email.
  • It stays until you leave. Every email has a link to leave, it takes one click, and leaving deletes the entry.
  • The Sunday email has one tiny image, the same in every copy, that adds one to a count of how often that week's email was opened. Links to this site in our emails, and in plans you send someone, carry a tag saying which email or that it was shared, the same for everyone. Neither says who you are, and we never track what any one person opens or taps.

On your phone

The age, nap, wake-up time and area you pick are saved in your own browser so the site opens where you left it. They aren't sent to us. Our own count above sets no cookies. Its fonts and maps come from this site too.

Google Analytics

To see how the site is used, such as how many people visit, whether they come back, on a phone or a computer, and which pages lead to a sign-up, our pages load Google Analytics. It sets its own cookies and sends Google what pages you open, which of a few buttons you tap, and the words you search for on our search page; we see only totals, never who you are. Google's policy on how it uses data from sites applies. You can turn it off with Google's opt-out add-on or your browser's tracking protection. The family planner page loads it too, as set out under Family plans below.

Google Ads

We advertise on Google, so our pages load Google's ad tag. It tells Google when someone who tapped one of our ads visits or joins the Sunday email, so we can see which ads work. Google may set its own cookies to do that, and its policy on ads applies to them. You can turn off personalised ads at My Ad Center. The family planner page, which holds your family's details, doesn't load it.

Family plans

Family plans need an account, and your account keeps what you tell us so it's there on every phone and computer you sign in on: your email address, your home (the address, ZIP, town or location you give), your children's names, birth months and sleep times, and the days you save. We use it to make your plans and for nothing else. We don't sell it, share it, or use it for ads.

It's kept on our own server, sealed with a key only that server has. Your browser keeps a copy too, so the page opens quickly, and signing out removes that copy. Your browser works out your plans from the same published plans anyone can read, and reads each day's plans from this site for your children's age groups, so GitHub Pages, like any web host, can see that those files were asked for. If you tap "Use this phone's location", your phone or browser works that out, and its maker's location service may be involved.

When you type a street address, our server finds it on the map with the U.S. Census Bureau's free address lookup, which is sent the address and nothing else: not your name, email or anything about your family. The drive from your home to each outing is then worked out by our own road map, on our own server, and nobody else is asked.

You can also type an address or ZIP under a day's map without an account, to see that day's drives from it. An address is found the same way, a ZIP from our own list. Our server keeps neither; your browser keeps the place it found so the other days' maps can use it, until you remove it there.

Your account

  • You sign in with your email address and a password. We keep the password only in scrambled form (a salted hash), so we can't read it. When you make an account we email you a link and a 6-digit code to confirm the address, and the account is only made once you use one. If you forget your password, we email you the same to sign in and choose a new one. Either works once, for 15 minutes. These emails go through Postmark, which keeps its own records of messages sent.
  • You stay signed in on a device until you sign out, or until you haven't used it for 180 days.
  • If you start a plan, your email address is given to Stripe's checkout so you don't have to type it again.
  • The family page loads Google Analytics (not Google's ad tag). While you're signed in, it gives Google a number made by scrambling your account a second time, so we can see how families use the page across their phones and computers, and it tells Google when you sign in, go to checkout, and use the planner (add a child or grown-up, plan around an event, type a request, save a day), by what you did and nothing about it. When you go to checkout, the page also gives our server Google Analytics' own random number for your browser, kept with your plan, so our server can tell Google when Stripe says your trial started, a payment went through or the plan was cancelled, with the plan and the amount. It never sends your email, your home, your children's details or your plans.
  • If you stop using an account that never paid, it's deleted after a year. You can delete your account yourself at any time on the family page, once any plan is cancelled, and everything in it is deleted with it.

Emails when a saved plan changes

These are off until you turn them on. Then each morning we check your saved plans against that day's published plans and email you, once, if a plan is no longer listed, has moved, or has bad weather forecast. A plan is dropped once its day has passed. You can turn them off on the family page. The emails go through Postmark.

Typed requests

When you type a request, like "somewhere indoors near Cary after 3", our server sends what you typed, today's date and the days we have plans for to Anthropic's Claude, which reads it as settings for the planner: the day, the time, indoors or out, a town, and the kind of outing. Nothing else goes with it: not your name, email, home or anything about your children. Anthropic doesn't use it to train its models and keeps it only for a short time under its commercial terms. Your browser then works out the day from the published plans, as always. We don't keep what you typed, only how many requests you've made this month.

Paying

Stripe handles payment. It keeps your email, name, address and card, and we never see the card. We keep whether your plan is active, its renewal date and Stripe's reference for it, for as long as you have the account. Stripe keeps its own records of payments, as the law requires.

Carrying plans to Tot Routines

When you make a code, your plans are sealed on your device with that code. We hold the sealed copy for up to 15 minutes, can't open it because we never see the code, and delete it as soon as it's collected. The code also links that phone to your account: we keep a scrambled copy of the phone's key (never the key itself), and with it the app asks for your saved days each time it opens, so a day you save or remove here changes there too. Those are the same saved days your account already keeps. We don't know which phone it is or anything else about it, and Unlink under Saved plans, or deleting your account, ends it. Inside the app your plans are shared with your family's phones sealed, like everything else in Tot Routines.

A family plan includes Supporter, so your code also carries a key that unlocks it in the app. If you share large videos, our server can then tell that your plan and one Tot Routines family go together. It still can't read anything that family keeps.

Other sites

The site itself is hosted by GitHub Pages, which keeps its own server records as any web host does. Links to an event's page or to Google Maps take you to those sites, and their own policies apply there.
